## Racing leagues

### Drone Racing League (DRL), the closed league

- DRL was founded in 2015 by Nicholas Horbaczewski and is privately held and headquartered in New York City, per the company's own boilerplate used across its press releases. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-drone-racing-league-and-the-united-states-air-force-announce-groundbreaking-initiative-to-elevate-women-in-sports-and-technology-302093772.html [verified] (primary, company boilerplate repeated across many PR Newswire releases)
- DRL launched publicly in January 2016 with its first season. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League and https://www.thembsgroup.co.uk/external/lux-capital-among-the-firms-backing-drone-racing-league-in-20m-funding-round/ [verified]
- Horbaczewski came to DRL from Tough Mudder, where he was Chief Revenue Officer, so DRL was built by an events/marketing operator rather than by an FPV pilot. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single]
- DRL's model is a closed league: all pilots fly identical league-built spec aircraft, which removes hardware advantage and makes the racing a pure pilot contest, and also means no consumer hardware ecosystem is attached to it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single]
- DRL's spec airframes by generation: Racer2 (2016), Racer3 (2017 to 2018), Racer4 (2019 onward), plus RacerX, a one-off record aircraft. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single]
- RacerX set a Guinness World Record for fastest ground speed by a battery-powered remote-controlled quadcopter, officially recorded at 163.5 mph with a top run of 179.6 mph, in 2017. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single] (needs the Guinness record page to promote to verified)
- DRL season champions: 2016 Jordan "Jet" Temkin, 2017 Jordan "Jet" Temkin, 2018 Paul "Nurk" Nurkkala, 2019 Alex Vanover. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single]
- The 2020 DRL season was disrupted by COVID-19, and DRL ran a "2020 LIVE Racing Season" with new consumer sponsors (BODYARMOR, Champion Athleticwear, RESPAWN) announced November 2020. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/drone-racing-league-partners-with-bodyarmor-champion-athleticwear-and-respawn-as-2020-live-racing-season-begins-301178008.html [verified] (primary press release)
- The FAA accredited DRL as its first UAS event organizer in December 2021, a regulatory status no community league held at the time. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-federal-aviation-administration-accredits-the-drone-racing-league-as-first-uas-event-organizer-301439874.html [verified] (primary press release)

### MultiGP, the open community league

- MultiGP was founded in February 2015, with Chris Thomas as founder and CEO, headquartered in Palm Bay, Florida. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP [single]
- MultiGP is a Special Interest Group (SIG) of the Academy of Model Aeronautics (AMA), which is what gives its chapters an insurance and field-access path in the United States. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP [single]
- MultiGP's structural unit is the chapter: a local, self-organising club that runs its own races under MultiGP rules, which is the opposite of DRL's single centrally produced circuit. https://www.multigp.com/chapters/ [verified] (primary, league's own chapters page)
- MultiGP runs a qualification ladder: local chapter races, then the Universal Time Trial and Global Qualifier events feeding a global leaderboard, then the International Open championship. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP [single]
- MultiGP International Open / National Championship winners by year: 2015 Shaun "Nytfury" Taylor (Orlando FL), 2016 Shaun Taylor (Muncie IN), 2017 Alex "CaptainVanover" Vanover (Reno NV), 2018 Evan "HeadsUpFPV" Turner (Las Vegas NV), 2019 Evan Turner (Daytona Beach FL), 2020 Soren "Phatkid" Monroe-Anderson (Melbourne FL), 2021 Evan Turner, 2022 MinChan Kim (KOR), 2023 MinChan Kim (KOR), 2024 Pawel "Pawelos" Laszczak (POL). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP [single] (single secondary source, needs MultiGP's own results pages to promote)
- The same names appear at the top of both ladders: Alex Vanover won MultiGP in 2017 and the DRL season in 2019, and Evan Turner won MultiGP three times, so the two systems shared a talent pool rather than being separate sports. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_Racing_League [single] (two Wikipedia articles are not independent sources, the cross-check only confirms internal consistency)

### The US Nationals and the Drone Sports Association

- The first U.S. National Drone Racing Championships were held at the California State Fair in 2015, a two day event with a $25,000 cash prize that attracted over 120 competitors. https://www.nytimes.com/2015/11/12/sports/drone-racing-competition.html and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single] (Wikipedia citing NYT, NYT text not directly captured here)
- Chad Nowak, an Australian, won the 2015 US Nationals, taking all three events: individual time trial, team trial and freestyle trick competition. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- The 2015 event was branded the Fat Shark National Drone Racing Championships, which is an early example of an FPV hardware vendor, not a broadcaster, paying for the sport. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- The 2016 U.S. National Drone Racing Championships were held on Governors Island, New York City, 5 to 7 August 2016, presented by GoPro, with 150 pilots selected through 20 regional qualifiers from a pool of 1,400 competitors, and $50,000 in prize money. https://www.forbes.com/sites/janetwburns/2016/08/08/national-drone-racing-finals-have-espn-big-finance-flocking-to-the-sport/ [single] (Wikipedia gives 145 pilots and $57,000, so the exact figures are contested, see Unknowns)
- ESPN3 streamed the 2016 US Nationals live, the first drone racing event broadcast by ESPN. https://www.forbes.com/sites/janetwburns/2016/08/08/national-drone-racing-finals-have-espn-big-finance-flocking-to-the-sport/ [single]
- The 2016 US Nationals were hosted by the Drone Sports Association (DSA), chaired by Dr Scot Refsland, a for-profit body distinct from the community-run MultiGP. https://www.forbes.com/sites/janetwburns/2016/08/08/national-drone-racing-finals-have-espn-big-finance-flocking-to-the-sport/ and https://espnpressroom.com/press-release/idra-and-espn-announce-drone-racing-international-media-distribution-partnership/ [verified] (Forbes plus ESPN's own press release naming DSA)
- The 2016 Nationals sponsor list, GoPro, Ernst & Young, AIG and EMC, shows the hype peak clearly: the money came from insurers and consultancies buying an innovation story, not from endemic drone businesses. https://www.forbes.com/sites/janetwburns/2016/08/08/national-drone-racing-finals-have-espn-big-finance-flocking-to-the-sport/ [single]
- As of August 2016, DRL had raised $8 million, so at the hype peak DRL was still a small startup competing with DSA to become "the NASCAR of flying robots". https://www.forbes.com/sites/janetwburns/2016/08/08/national-drone-racing-finals-have-espn-big-finance-flocking-to-the-sport/ [single]

### DR1 Racing and the Drone Champions League

- DR1 Racing ran the 2017 DHL Champions Series, six races worldwide. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- The DR1 Isle of Man TT Finals aired on CBS and Eurosport and drew a 0.4 share and 559,000 viewers, described as the largest network television audience for professional drone racing at that time. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- That 559,000 figure is the single most useful number in this whole file: it is the ceiling that broadcast drone racing actually reached on a major US network, against a hype narrative that promised a mainstream sport. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- The Drone Champions League (DCL) fielded teams of at least four pilots each by 2018 and broadcast races live. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing and https://dcl.aero/watch/livestream/ [single]

### FAI and national teams

- The first FAI World Drone Racing Championship was held in Shenzhen, China in 2018, with over 128 competitors from 34 countries, and it aired on the Olympic Channel. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single]
- The 2018 FAI world title was won by a 17 year old Australian (Rudi Browning). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing [single] (name needs an FAI results page to confirm)
- The 2019 FAI World Drone Racing Championship was held in Xiangshan, Ningbo, China, won by Changhyeon Kang of South Korea, beating former champion Thomas Bitmatta. https://www.fai.org/news/korea-flies-victory-fai-world-drone-racing-championship-grand-final-2019 [single]
- FAI also runs a Drone Racing World Cup series separate from the World Championship: MinJae Kim of South Korea won the 2022 World Cup at age fourteen, and a 2023 Korea Drone Racing World Cup was held in Incheon. https://www.fai.org/news/hard-work-pays-2022-fai-drone-racing-world-cup-champion-minjae-kim and https://www.fai.org/news/2023-fai-korea-drone-racing-world-cup-incheon [verified] (two primary FAI news items)
- South Korea's dominance of the FAI results (2019 and 2022 champions, and MinChan Kim winning MultiGP in 2022 and 2023) is the clearest evidence that competitive racing's centre of gravity moved to Asia while the media economy stayed in the US. https://www.fai.org/news/korea-flies-victory-fai-world-drone-racing-championship-grand-final-2019 and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MultiGP [single] (interpretation)

### DRL's exit and its state in 2026

- On 23 April 2024 Infinite Reality ("iR") announced a definitive agreement to acquire the Drone Racing League for $250 million, which iR said took its own valuation from $3 billion to $3.5 billion. https://www.einpresswire.com/article/705838339/infinite-reality-acquires-the-drone-racing-league-for-250-million-solidifying-position-as-global-leader-in-immersive-technology-experiences and https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2024/04/23/drone-racing-league-acquires-by-infinite-reality/ [verified] (issuer release plus trade press; also filed as an SEC exhibit at https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1831978/000121390024035122/ea020443401ex99-1_new.htm)

### DRL broadcast

- ESPN got to drone racing through the community side first: on 13 April 2016 the Drone Sports Association and ESPN announced a multi-year international media distribution deal covering the 2016 US National Drone Racing Championships and the 2016 World Drone Racing Championships, with live streaming on ESPN3 and one-hour televised specials after each event on an ESPN network. Financial terms were not disclosed. https://espnpressroom.com/press-release/idra-and-espn-announce-drone-racing-international-media-distribution-partnership/ [verified] (primary, ESPN press room)
- On 14 September 2016 DRL announced multi-year broadcast distribution with ESPN, Sky and ProSiebenSat.1 plus a content partnership with MGM Television and Mark Burnett. https://us.espnpressroom.com/press-releases/2016/09/drone-racing-league-drl-announces-u-s-international-broadcast-distribution-espn-sky-prosiebensat-1-content-partnership-mgm-television/ and https://www.si.com/media/2016/10/01/drone-racing-league-espn-tv [verified] (ESPN press room plus Sports Illustrated; the ESPN page itself returned an error on fetch, the SI piece carries the same terms)
- The ESPN deal was a 10-episode package for the 2016 fall season, built around profiles of 25 pilots plus races in Detroit, Los Angeles and Miami. Note the shape: it was a documentary series about pilots, not live sport. https://www.si.com/media/2016/10/01/drone-racing-league-espn-tv [single]
- Horbaczewski said at the time that "how many viewers will actually tune in for a new spectacle remains to be seen", and credited esports with making the conversations with executives easier. The league's own founder framed the TV bet as unproven in 2016. https://www.si.com/media/2016/10/01/drone-racing-league-espn-tv [single]
- ESPN's own programming director framed it as "storytelling, technology and competition into compelling weekly content", again a content buy rather than a rights buy. https://www.si.com/media/2016/10/01/drone-racing-league-espn-tv [single]
- DRL's first major consumer sponsorship was Bud Light, announced 11 November 2016. https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2016/11/11/Technology/drone-racing-league-announces-first-major-partnership-with-bud-light/ [single]
- DRL moved its US broadcast rights from ESPN to NBC in March 2019, and announced the 2019 season with Twitter and NBC. https://www.adweek.com/digital/the-drone-racing-league-moves-its-broadcast-rights-from-espn-to-nbc/ and https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-drone-racing-league-launches-2019-season-with-twitter-and-nbc-300810554.html [verified] (trade press plus primary release)

## Culture shift

- DRL's own audience numbers are digital, not broadcast: for the 2022 to 2023 Algorand World Championship season DRL reported 320 million households reached, 260 million global digital views, 12 million social fans and 3 million video views per day. https://www.thedronegirl.com/2023/06/21/2023-drone-racing-audience-stats/ [single] (league-supplied figures, reported by The Drone Girl, article dated 18 May 2024)
- By the April 2024 acquisition, DRL claimed over one billion annual digital video views, distribution to 320 million households, 15 million social followers including 5.4 million TikTok followers. https://www.einpresswire.com/article/705838339/infinite-reality-acquires-the-drone-racing-league-for-250-million-solidifying-position-as-global-leader-in-immersive-technology-experiences [single] (acquirer press release, marketing figures)
- Set those against the one independently reported TV rating available, 559,000 viewers for the DR1 Isle of Man final on CBS: the sport's real reach was social video, and the broadcast product never found an audience proportional to the money spent on it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_racing and https://www.thedronegirl.com/2023/06/21/2023-drone-racing-audience-stats/ [single] (interpretation from two sourced numbers)
- The structural problem is visible in the numbers rather than in opinion: a "household reach" figure counts channel carriage, a "digital views" figure counts short-form clips, and neither is an audience for a race. No source found in this research publishes a per-race TV rating for DRL. See Unknowns. [single]
- DRL's YouTube channel holds 1.55 million subscribers and 1.19 billion lifetime views, created 16 April 2015. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@thedroneracingleague/ [single] (vidIQ, stats updated 11 August 2026)
- Mr Steele, a freestyle pilot who has never run a league, holds 561,000 YouTube subscribers and 107.43 million lifetime views across 860 videos. So a single freestyle pilot's channel is roughly a third of the entire professional league's subscriber base and about 9 percent of its lifetime views, on no capital at all. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@MrSteeleFPV/ and https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@thedroneracingleague/ [single] (both vidIQ, stats updated 11 August 2026)
- Joshua Bardwell, who is an educator rather than a competitor, holds 435,000 subscribers and 117.96 million lifetime views, MORE lifetime views than Mr Steele and more than twice Rotor Riot's. Education outperformed both racing and freestyle content. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@joshuabardwell/ [single] (vidIQ, stats updated 10 August 2026)
- Racing did keep a participation base while losing the media narrative: MultiGP states over 30,000 registered pilots and 500 active chapters worldwide. https://www.multigp.com/chapters/ [verified] (primary, league's own page, captured 12 August 2026)
- MultiGP's 2024 Championship drew over 900 pilots through the Global Qualifier season, with 120 pilots from 15 countries qualifying to the championship itself, held 22 to 27 October 2024 at No Quarter Ranch, Citrus Springs, Florida. https://www.multigp.com/2024-multigp-championship-recap/ [verified] (primary, league's own recap)
- The 2024 MultiGP PRO winner Pawel "Pawelos" Laszczak took a $7,000 cash prize. Compare the Dubai 2016 team purse of $250,000: top-level open drone racing prize money fell by roughly an order of magnitude in eight years. https://www.multigp.com/2024-multigp-championship-recap/ and https://apnews.com/international-news-general-news-519ad8518cde4d888b59c435294b7a5d [verified] (two primary or wire sources)
- 2024 was the first time in MultiGP Championship history that the top four were entirely international with no American pilots in the semifinals, which marks the end of US dominance in the open racing ladder. https://www.multigp.com/2024-multigp-championship-recap/ [verified] (primary)

Open community racing is still running annual national-scale events in 2026. https://www.multigp.com/io26/ [verified] (primary, captured 12 August 2026)
- The Drone Champions League announced a 2026 season with a three-cup format starting in Riyadh, so European team-based racing also survived. https://dronechampionsleague.com/dcl-season-2026/ [single]

### Cinematic FPV going mainstream

- CORRECTION to a common dating: the Jay Christensen bowling alley video is a 2021 video, not 2020. "Right Up Our Alley" debuted 8 March 2021. https://www.wrtv.com/watch-bowling-alley-drone-video-impressing-hollywood/ and https://www.cined.com/fpv-drone-flying-at-its-finest-right-up-our-alley-is-an-87sec-masterpiece/ [verified] (two independent outlets, one dated 15 March 2021)
- The video is 87 seconds, shot in a single take at Bryant Lake Bowl and Theater, Minneapolis, flown by Jay Christensen and directed by Anthony Jaska of Rally Studios. https://www.rally-studios.com/news/a-drone-went-bowling-hollywood-noticed [verified] (primary, production company's own account)
- It was flown on a cinewhoop, a small ducted FPV quad, with a GoPro Hero 8 recording to SD card alongside the analog FPV camera feeding the pilot's goggles. https://www.cined.com/fpv-drone-flying-at-its-finest-right-up-our-alley-is-an-87sec-masterpiece/ [single]
- The single take took several attempts, and all audio is post-production sound design because the original capture would only have propeller noise. https://www.cined.com/fpv-drone-flying-at-its-finest-right-up-our-alley-is-an-87sec-masterpiece/ [single]
- It was made pro bono, commissioned by Rally Studios itself to draw support for businesses hurt by the COVID-19 pandemic. The most influential FPV commercial video ever made was an unpaid job. https://www.rally-studios.com/news/a-drone-went-bowling-hollywood-noticed [verified] (primary)
- View trajectory: 1.6 million views within days of the 8 March 2021 debut, "nearly 2 million" shortly after, and 6.4 million at the time of the Hollywood Reporter piece. https://www.cined.com/fpv-drone-flying-at-its-finest-right-up-our-alley-is-an-87sec-masterpiece/ and https://www.wrtv.com/watch-bowling-alley-drone-video-impressing-hollywood/ and https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/how-a-viral-video-about-a-bowling-alley-led-to-a-job-offer-from-james-gunn-4151955/ [verified] (three outlets; the HR body is paywalled to this tool, the 6.4M figure is from its search-indexed summary)
- Direct commercial consequence: James Gunn called the work "stupendous" and invited Rally Studios to join his crew in London for Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3. https://www.rally-studios.com/news/a-drone-went-bowling-hollywood-noticed and https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/how-a-viral-video-about-a-bowling-alley-led-to-a-job-offer-from-james-gunn-4151955/ [verified]
- Michael Bay's "Ambulance", released 8 April 2022, is the first major studio feature to build action sequences around FPV drone photography, flown by Alex Vanover, with a behind-the-scenes featurette released by Universal Pictures. https://dronedj.com/2022/03/25/michael-bay-fpv-drone-ambulance/ and https://ymcinema.com/2022/03/28/ambulance-fpv-drone-featurette-with-the-komodo-bayhem/ [verified]
- The heavy shots on Ambulance were flown on a cinelifter, an FPV quad large enough to carry a cinema camera, reported as a RED Komodo. https://dronexl.co/2022/04/06/how-the-fpv-drone-shots-ambulance/ and https://ymcinema.com/2022/03/28/ambulance-fpv-drone-featurette-with-the-komodo-bayhem/ [verified]
- Enabling software: GoPro acquired ReelSteady on 13 March 2020, bringing founders Robert McIntosh and Andy Russell in-house. ReelSteady's post-production stabilisation is what turned raw freestyle GoPro footage into deliverable cinematography. Deal terms were not disclosed. https://gopro.com/en/us/news/reelsteady-joins-gopro-team and https://www.personal-view.com/news/articles/gopro-acquires-reelsteady-stabilization-software-company [verified]
- Enabling hardware: DJI launched the DJI FPV on 2 March 2021 at $1,299 for the standard combo (drone, controller, Goggles V2, one battery). Note that the widely quoted "$999 DJI FPV" is wrong for the 2021 model. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/dji-reinvents-the-drone-flying-experience-with-the-dji-fpv-301238218.html and https://dronedj.com/2021/03/02/heres-djis-full-news-release-for-its-new-fpv-drone-combo/ [verified] (primary DJI release plus trade coverage)
- DJI Avata launched August 2022 from $629 for the aircraft alone up to $1,388 for a full combo, DJI's first cinewhoop-style product. https://dronexl.co/2022/08/09/dji-avata-pricing-drone-specifications/ and https://dronedj.com/2022/08/25/dji-avata-features-buy-price/ [verified]
- DJI Avata 2 launched April 2024 with a Fly More Combo including Goggles 3 and RC Motion 3 at $999. https://www.thedronegirl.com/2024/04/11/avata-2-review/ [single]
- Labour recognition followed the technique: in June 2024, after an arbitration win against Warner Bros, major studios agreed to cover drone camera crews under IATSE Local 600 (International Cinematographers Guild) contracts, giving drone operators union benefits, pension and health coverage on union productions. https://dronexl.co/2024/06/13/drone-camera-crews-win-union-coverage/ and https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/drone-camera-crews-now-covered-under-union-agreement-1235922171/ [verified]
- The commercial FPV service layer now advertises itself in regulatory and union terms rather than in pilot-skill terms: Part 107 certification, IATSE Local 600 membership, $10M insurance, and FAA waivers for night, over people and moving vehicles. https://weflyaerialmedia.com/local-600-drone-operator and https://www.wearegungho.com/frequently-asked-questions [verified] (two operator sites, primary self-description)
- Paul Nurkkala, the 2018 DRL champion, now lists an Emmy Award and sells cinematic drone filming and training, and Alex Vanover, the 2019 DRL champion, flew a Michael Bay feature. The exit route from competitive racing is film work, in both documented cases. https://paulnurkkala.com/ and https://dronedj.com/2022/03/25/michael-bay-fpv-drone-ambulance/ [verified]

## Media economy

### The affiliate and sponsorship model, documented from primary pages

- The funding model of FPV education is stated openly on Bardwell's own support page: subscribe on YouTube, subscribe on Patreon at any amount (which also grants Discord access), click an affiliate link before buying, or pay directly by PayPal or crypto. https://www.fpvknowitall.com/support-me/ [verified] (primary, captured 12 August 2026)
- Bardwell lists 14 or more affiliate partners on one page: RaceDayQuads, GetFPV, Rotor Riot, NewBeeDrone, Camera Butter, WeBleedFPV, Five33, GEPRC, BetaFPV, Caddx, Emax USA, Flywoo, HDZero, iFlight, MakerFire, RadioMaster. The list is the FPV supply chain, so the education layer is funded by the retail and manufacturer layer it reviews. https://www.fpvknowitall.com/support-me/ [verified] (primary)
- The conflict is structural and disclosed rather than hidden: the affiliate pitch is that it "costs you nothing more than a click", and the reviewer's income rises with sales of the products he reviews. No commission rates are published on the page. https://www.fpvknowitall.com/support-me/ [verified] (primary, direct observation)
- Oscar Liang's blog runs the same model in text form: affiliate links plus a Patreon, plus banner advertising sold directly. https://oscarliang.com/about/ [verified] (primary, captured 12 August 2026)
- oscarliang.com states over 1,500 blog posts and approximately 400,000 monthly page views, and Liang states his professional background has nothing to do with drones, "this is just my weekend hobby". https://oscarliang.com/about/ [verified] (primary, self-reported traffic, not an audited figure)
- Liang's Patreon starts at $2 per month. https://www.patreon.com/oscarliang [single]
- Liang publishes roughly 120 blog posts a year by his own statement, which is what keeps a text site ranking against YouTube. https://www.patreon.com/oscarliang/about [single]
- UAVfutures is Australian, channel created 4 June 2015, and built its audience on cheap-build guides, for instance "How to build a Pro FPV Racing DRONE for ONLY $99" at 1.7 million views. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@uavfutures/ and https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLP0PoGwU8OPba3a67C0ftS25xJNG_Hp43 [verified]
- UAVfutures: 218,000 subscribers, 44.04 million lifetime views, 1,330 videos. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@uavfutures/ [single] (vidIQ, stats updated 10 August 2026)
- UAVfutures also runs diyfpv.com, a community site with a product database, so the education channel became a platform business. https://www.diyfpv.com/profile/uavfutures [single]
- vidIQ's estimated monthly AdSense earnings for these channels are small: about $4,000 for Bardwell, $348 for UAVfutures, $289 for Mr Steele, $280 for Rotor Riot. Those estimates are unreliable in absolute terms, but their order of magnitude supports the conclusion that ad revenue alone cannot fund a full-time FPV channel, and affiliate plus Patreon plus courses must carry it. https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@joshuabardwell/ and https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@uavfutures/ and https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@MrSteeleFPV/ and https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@RotorRiot/ [single] (third-party estimates, explicitly not disclosed earnings)

so all sourcing was done by direct WebFetch. DuckDuckGo's HTML endpoint
CAPTCHAs aggressively and Bing returned locale-mismatched results, so most

blocked to this tool, which is why several bullets above could not be promoted
