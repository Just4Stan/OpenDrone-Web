## Numbers I distrust and why

- The entire cluster of "FPV drone market" reports listed above: none publishes what a unit is, whether goggles and air units count, whether DJI Avata counts as FPV, or whether military FPV procurement is included. Given that Ukraine alone contracted over 2 million FPV drones for 2025, a report claiming a total FPV market of 287 million USD and a report claiming 3.67 billion USD could both be internally consistent under different definitions and neither would tell you anything about the hobby. [verified] (definitions checked as absent)
- Verified Market Research and Verified Market Reports are separate publishers with confusingly similar names publishing incompatible figures for the same named market, 3.67 billion USD versus 1.6 billion USD. Any article citing "Verified Market" without the exact publisher name is unusable. [verified]
- Verified Market Reports internally contradicts itself: FPV racing at 1.2 billion USD in 2025 against all FPV at 1.6 billion USD in 2025 implies racing is 75 percent of the FPV market, which contradicts everything known about the hobby, where freestyle, cinematic and long range vastly outnumber racing. [verified] (arithmetic over the publisher's own two reports)
- The 83.48 percent Dedrone figure is the clearest example of a laundered number: it is a detection share from counter-drone sensors in conflict and security contexts, and it circulates as "DJI's market share". [verified]
- Second-tier statistics aggregators recycle each other. ElectroIQ and Coolest Gadgets both attribute DJI share figures to "Loyalty Drone Research", a source I could not locate, and they disagree with each other: ElectroIQ says about 70 percent of the global civilian market, Coolest Gadgets says 80 percent of the US market and 60 percent globally. https://electroiq.com/stats/dji-statistics/ https://coolest-gadgets.com/dji-statistics-by-usage-industry-market-and-sales-analysis-2025/ [verified] (the contradiction is verifiable even though the underlying source is not)
- The widely repeated "DJI has 70 to 80 percent of the consumer drone market" claim could not be traced to an original methodology. Every instance found points to another secondary source or to nothing. Treat as folklore with a plausible order of magnitude, not as data. [verified] (traced and failed)
- Any figure for DJI's share of the FPV segment specifically: I found none, from any source. Given that the traditional build-it-yourself FPV supply chain runs through iFlight, T-Motor, Foxeer, RunCam, BetaFPV, Happymodel and TBS, and that DJI competes there only via the O3/O4 air units, goggles and Avata, the consumer-camera-drone share figures should never be reused for FPV. ## DJI

### Founding and corporate story

- DJI was established on 18 January 2006 by Frank Wang, Wang Tao, who had enrolled at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology in 2003 and competed with the HKUST team in the ABU Robocon competition, winning third prize. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- The company began as a flight control component business, not a drone maker: Wang "built the first prototypes of DJI's projects in his dorm room, selling the flight control components to universities and Chinese electric companies". This matters for FPV because DJI's origin is the same flight-controller problem that Betaflight solves. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- Wang relocated to Shenzhen in 2006 after securing 90,000 USD from family friend Lu Di. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- The Phantom launched in 2013 at 629 USD and is described as the first commercially successful recreational drone. The Phantom 3 in 2015 added a built-in live streaming camera and made DJI the largest consumer drone company in the world. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- DJI is private and has never listed. It raised roughly 1 billion USD in 2018 in preparation for an envisioned Hong Kong Stock Exchange IPO that has not occurred. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- On state ownership, the contested point: a 2015 round of 500 million USD included state-owned New China Life Insurance, GIC and New Horizon Capital, and DJI has also received investment from SDIC Unity Capital, owned by the State Development and Investment Corporation, and China Chengtong Holdings Group, owned by SASAC. DJI's own position is that state-affiliated investors hold less than six percent of ownership and less than one percent of voting rights. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single] (both the investments and DJI's rebuttal come from the same secondary compilation, a primary cap table was not obtained)

## US policy

### Entity List, December 2020

- BIS added DJI to the Entity List in a final rule published 22 December 2020, document 2020-28031, effective 18 December 2020, in a batch that added 77 entities under 78 entries. https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2020-28031.json [verified] (primary: Federal Register)
- The stated justification was not military: the rule says the End-User Review Committee determined to add "AGCU Scientech; China National Scientific Instruments and Materials (CNSIM); DJI; and Kuang-Chi Group for activities contrary to U.S. foreign policy interests", specifically that "these four entities have enabled wide-scale human rights abuses within China through abusive genetic collection and analysis or high-technology surveillance". https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2020/12/22/2020-28031.txt [verified] (primary)
- The license requirement imposed on DJI is "All items subject to the EAR", with a review policy of "Case-by-case review for items necessary to detect, identify and treat infectious disease; Presumption of denial for all other items subject to the EAR". https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2020/12/22/2020-28031.txt [verified] (primary)
- The Entity List restricts the direction of trade that most coverage gets backwards: the rule states the EAR "impose additional license requirements on, and limit the availability of most license exceptions for, exports, reexports, and transfers (in-country) to listed entities". It restricts US-origin goods and technology flowing TO DJI. It is not an import ban and by itself never made it illegal to buy or sell a DJI product in the US. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2020/12/22/2020-28031.txt [verified] (primary)

### Section 1260H, Chinese military companies

- Section 1260H of the FY2021 NDAA (Pub. L. 116-283) is the authority under which the Department of Defense publishes its list of "Chinese military companies" operating in the United States. https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-11571.json [verified] (primary)
- The most recent 1260H notice in the Federal Register as of this research is document 2026-11571, published 10 June 2026, which names 108 entities and includes Shenzhen DJI Technology Co., Ltd., Autel Robotics Co., Ltd. and Autel Intelligent Technology Corp., Ltd. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/06/10/2026-11571.txt [verified] (primary)
- Prior 1260H notices of availability appear at 2 April 2024 (document 2024-06895) and 7 January 2025 (document 2025-00070), so the list is refreshed roughly annually. https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions%5Bterm%5D=DJI%20entity%20list [verified] (primary index)

### BIS ICTS rulemaking on unmanned aircraft, the one that could actually reach parts

- BIS published an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, "Securing the Information and Communications Technology and Services Supply Chain: Unmanned Aircraft Systems", document 2024-30209, published 3 January 2025, comments closing 4 March 2025, under Executive Order 13873. https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2024-30209.json [verified] (primary)
- The ANPRM scopes the ICTS components it considers integral to UAS as: onboard computers, communications systems including flight controllers, transceivers and GNSS sensors, flight control systems, ground control stations including handheld controllers, operating software, mission planning software, intelligent battery systems, data storage, and AI software. That component list maps almost exactly onto an FPV bill of materials. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/01/03/2024-30209.txt [verified] (primary)
- The foreign adversaries named in the ANPRM are China including Hong Kong, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, and the Maduro regime in Venezuela. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/01/03/2024-30209.txt [verified] (primary)
- The ANPRM sets no size or weight threshold and contains no hobbyist, recreational or model aircraft carve-out. It acknowledges that "UAS used in these sectors often rely on the same aircraft used by recreational drone enthusiasts" but does not exempt them. It also does not address whether rules would apply to components sold separately, which is exactly the question that matters for the FPV parts trade. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/01/03/2024-30209.txt [verified] (primary)

### Section 1709 of the FY2025 NDAA and the December 2025 deadline, the single most important item in this file

- Section 1709 of the Servicemember Quality of Life Improvement and National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2025, Pub. L. 118-159, 138 Stat. 1773, 2209-10 (2024), directs the FCC to add certain covered UAS communications and video surveillance equipment to the Covered List if "an appropriate national security agency" determines it poses unacceptable risk. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary: FCC quoting the statute)
- The statute named DJI and Autel but did not assign any specific agency to perform the review, a drafting gap DJI publicly complained about: DJI wrote on 20 November 2025 that the audit "has not begun" and that "the NDAA did not assign any U.S. agency to actually conduct the audit". https://viewpoints.dji.com/blog/whats-going-on-with-dji-in-the-u.s [single] (company statement, self-interested)
- DJI sent letters on 1 December 2025 to DHS, DoD, the FBI, the NSA and ODNI asking for the audit to be performed, citing six prior audits by Booz Allen Hamilton, FTI Consulting, TUV SUD and Idaho National Laboratory. https://dronedj.com/2025/12/08/dji-letters-us-drone-ban/ [single] (trade press, named reporter Ishveena Singh)
- The operative deadline was 23 December 2025, after which Covered List placement would follow. https://dronedj.com/2025/12/08/dji-letters-us-drone-ban/ https://viewpoints.dji.com/blog/whats-going-on-with-dji-in-the-u.s [verified] (two independent sources)
- What actually happened: no agency completed the audit. Instead, on 21 December 2025 the White House convened an Executive Branch interagency body which issued a National Security Determination, and on 22 December 2025 the FCC Public Safety and Homeland Security Bureau issued Public Notice DA 25-1086 adding to the Covered List, in dockets WC 18-89, ET 21-232 and EA 21-233. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The scope is far wider than DJI. The Covered List entry added on 22 December 2025 reads: "Uncrewed aircraft systems (UAS) and UAS critical components produced in a foreign country and all communications and video surveillance equipment and services listed in Section 1709(a)(1) of the FY25 National Defense Authorization Act (Pub. L. 118-159)." https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The FCC states explicitly that this entry is not entity based: "this newly-covered equipment is identified by place of production, not by entity". It therefore reaches every foreign producer, not only Chinese ones, and on its face reaches European and other allied production too. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The National Security Determination defines "UAS critical components" as including but not limited to: data transmission devices, communications systems, flight controllers, ground control stations and UAS controllers, navigation systems, sensors and cameras, batteries and battery management systems, and motors, plus any associated software. That enumeration is effectively a complete FPV bill of materials. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The Determination's stated goal is industrial, not merely security: "UAS and UAS critical components must be produced in the United States", and "we must cease the importation of all new foreign-manufactured UAS and UAS critical components". https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The stated triggers include the 2026 FIFA World Cup and the 2028 Olympics, Executive Order 14305 "Restoring American Airspace Sovereignty" of June 2025, and Executive Order 14307 "Unleashing American Drone Dominance" of June 2025. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The determination cites Ukraine and Gaza directly as evidence: "In Ukraine and Israel-Gaza, low-cost commercial UAS inflict extensive damage and have caused significant loss of life", citing Bloomberg and Kyiv Post. This is a documented instance of the war changing regulation that touches the hobby supply chain. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The legal mechanism is equipment authorization, not possession: under 47 CFR 2.903(a) covered equipment is prohibited from receiving equipment authorizations, and under 47 CFR 2.911(d)(5)(i) applicants must certify their equipment is not covered. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The action is prospective. Drones already approved, sold or flying in the US are unaffected and owners can keep using them. https://dronedj.com/2025/12/22/dji-drone-us-fcc-ban/ https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified]
- Escape hatch: the Department of War or DHS can make a specific determination that a given UAS, class of UAS, or critical component does not pose the risk, and the FCC will then update the list. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The FCC began issuing Conditional Approvals in January 2026. As of 4 June 2026 eleven manufacturers had them, including SiFly Aviation, Mobilicom, ScoutDI, Verge, Sees.ai, Air6 System, Elevon Aerial, Blueflite, Verity AG, Air VEV and Innovation First International, most valid through 31 December 2026. No FPV hobby component maker appears on that list. https://dronedj.com/2026/06/09/fcc-drone-ban-exempt-list/ [single]
- In May 2026 the FCC extended a waiver, DA 26-454, allowing already-authorized DJI and Autel drones to keep receiving firmware and software updates until at least 1 January 2029, up from a previous January 2027 cutoff. https://dronedj.com/2026/05/18/dji-autel-fcc-drone-firmware/ [single]
- As of 7 August 2026 DJI states the FCC is "considering reversing that position and restricting cleared products as well", meaning already-authorized models could lose their status. Treat as DJI's characterisation pending the actual FCC item. https://viewpoints.dji.com/blog/the-fcc-could-cut-off-future-access-to-popular-dji-drone-models-in-the-u.s.-heres-what-you-can-do-about-it [single] (company statement)
- Industry group AUVSI supported the action, with president and CEO Michael Robbins quoted: "The FCC has built on the actions directed by Congress to protect US national security and encourage American Drone Dominance." https://dronedj.com/2025/12/23/auvsi-fcc-us-foreign-drone/ [single]

### What this means for hobby FPV parts

- ANALYSIS, not a sourced claim, flagged as such: the Covered List bites through equipment authorization, so its practical force falls on parts that need an FCC ID, that is VTXs, receivers, air units, radio transmitters and goggles. A brushless motor or a carbon frame needs no equipment authorization in the first place, so listing motors has no direct authorization effect on them even though the determination names motors. Someone should verify this against the FCC UAS Covered List FAQ, which I could not fetch. https://www.fcc.gov/covered-list-faqs-uas-and-uas-critical-components [could not verify, fcc.gov returned HTTP 403]
- The Entity List, the 1260H designation and the Treasury investment ban are all irrelevant to a hobbyist buying an iFlight frame or a Foxeer camera: none of them restrict imports or private purchases. The FCC Covered List and any future BIS ICTS rule are the two mechanisms that could actually reach hobby parts. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2020/12/22/2020-28031.txt https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary documents, reading of legal effect is mine)

## Chinese export controls

- MOFCOM Announcement 2023 No. 28, issued 31 July 2023 jointly by the Ministry of Commerce, the General Administration of Customs, the State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defence, and the Central Military Commission Equipment Development Department, imposed temporary export controls on certain UAVs and UAV equipment effective 1 September 2023. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [verified] (primary: MOFCOM)
- The 2023 threshold test is specific and worth quoting because it explains why hobby FPV gear largely fell outside it: controlled UAVs must be capable of controlled flight beyond the operator's line of sight, have maximum endurance of 30 minutes or more, and have maximum takeoff weight above 7 kg or empty weight above 4 kg. A 5 inch freestyle quad meets none of the weight criteria and rarely the endurance one. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [verified] (primary)
- On top of the size test, a controlled UAV must also have at least one of: onboard radio power exceeding international civil wireless certification limits, a payload throwing function or integrated thrower, a hyperspectral or multispectral camera outside the 560, 650, 730 and 860 nm bands, an infrared camera with NETD below 40 mK, a laser rangefinder above specified classes per GB7247.1-2012, or support for non-certified payloads. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [verified] (primary)
- The thermal imaging threshold, NETD below 40 mK, is the one that reaches consumer gear: it captures most decent thermal camera modules, which is why thermal became hard to buy internationally while ordinary FPV cameras did not. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [single] (primary text, inference is mine)
- The 2023 measure was explicitly temporary: "The temporary control implementation period shall not exceed two years". https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [verified] (primary)
- The announcement contains no exemption or carve-out language for consumer or civilian drones. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwgk/zcfb/art/2023/art_e73e4230d30542f9bd433ea20fc45b05.html [verified] (primary, absence checked)
- On 31 July 2024 MOFCOM revised the regime effective 1 September 2024: temporary controls on certain high performance consumer drones were dropped, high precision inertial measurement equipment was added, and controls on infrared thermal imaging devices, target indicating lasers and hyperspectral cameras were retained with revised standards. Reported by Reuters, 31 July 2024, "China revises drone export controls amid western scrutiny". https://www.reuters.com/ [single] (I have the Reuters headline and date but could not fetch the article itself, so treat the detail as trade press summary)
- Rare earth export controls are the control regime that actually touches every FPV motor. China imposed export licensing in April 2025 covering seven medium and heavy rare earth elements: samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium and yttrium, plus metals, oxides, alloys and downstream products including NdFeB magnets containing terbium or dysprosium and SmCo magnets. https://rare-earth-mining.com/china-rare-earth-export-controls/ [single] (secondary industry source, needs a MOFCOM primary citation)

## War impact on the industry

### Production scale, sourced, with target versus actual separated

- TARGET: President Zelensky stated in early 2025 that Ukraine was capable of producing 4 million drones annually. This is a capability statement, not an output figure. [single] (widely reported, primary statement not directly fetched)
- PROCUREMENT PLAN: Ukraine's Ministry of Defence planned to procure 4.5 million FPV drones in 2025, per Glib Kanievskyi, Director of Procurement Policy. Plan, not delivery. [single]
- BUDGET: 110 billion UAH, roughly 2.6 billion USD, allocated for drone procurement, of which 102 billion UAH was managed by the Defence Procurement Agency. Year stated as 2025. [single]
- CONTRACTED: the Defence Procurement Agency contracted over 2 million FPV drones for 2025. Contracted, not delivered. [single]
- ACTUAL DELIVERED: 1 million FPV drones supplied between January and July 2025, per the Ukrainian Ministry of Defence. This is the most defensible actual-output figure I found. https://mod.gov.ua/ [single] (attributed to the ministry, exact page not retrieved)
- INDUSTRY STRUCTURE: over 160 companies producing FPV drones in Ukraine. [single]
- Bloomberg reported on 11 November 2025 that Ukraine was producing around 4 million drones annually, exceeding combined NATO output. [single]
- A 2026 figure attributed to Ukraine's National Security and Defence Council puts defence industry capacity at more than 8 million FPV drones per year. Capacity, not output. [single]
- Monthly output is reported to have risen from roughly 20,000 units per month in 2024 to 200,000 per month in 2025. [single] (no named primary source obtained)
- IMPORTANT CAVEAT: all of the above except the January to July 2025 delivery figure are targets, plans, contracts or capacity claims. Reporting routinely presents them as production. Any article using these numbers must say which is which. [verified] (this is an observation about the sources themselves)

### Company statements and policy effects

- DJI suspended business in both Russia and Ukraine in April 2022, stating the intent to prevent its products being used in combat. Products nonetheless reached both sides via intermediaries. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI [single]
- The US government's own December 2025 National Security Determination cites the war as evidence: "In Ukraine and Israel-Gaza, low-cost commercial UAS inflict extensive damage and have caused significant loss of life", citing Bloomberg (Marissa Newman, December 2023) and Kyiv Post (Steve Brown, March 2025). This is the clearest documented line from the war to regulation affecting civilian parts. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- The same determination frames dual use explicitly: "UAS are inherently dual-use: they are both commercial platforms and potentially military or paramilitary sensors and weapons", and notes that risk applies "Even when marketed as 'commercial' or 'recreational'". That sentence is the policy basis for hobby gear being swept in. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- I could not verify: specific public statements by iFlight, T-Motor, Foxeer, RunCam, Walksnail, HDZero, BetaFPV or Happymodel about military use; any Betaflight, ExpressLRS, INAV or ArduPilot governance or licence response to military use; documented FPV hobby part price rises attributable to war demand with a named source. These are real gaps and all were on my list. [could not verify]

## EU / reshoring

- The US policy driver is now explicitly industrial. Executive Order 14307 "Unleashing American Drone Dominance", June 2025, establishes a policy of a resilient domestic UAS industrial base, and Executive Order 14305 "Restoring American Airspace Sovereignty", June 2025, calls to "scale up domestic production and expand the export of trusted, American-manufactured drone technologies to global markets". https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/06/unleashing-american-drone-dominance/ https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/06/restoring-american-airspace-sovereignty/ [verified] (cited verbatim in the FCC primary document)
- The December 2025 National Security Determination states the reshoring goal in the bluntest possible terms: "we must cease the importation of all new foreign-manufactured UAS and UAS critical components" and "the U.S. UAS industry cannot rely on foreign-produced UAS critical components". https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary)
- Note the trap for European makers: because the FCC entry is defined by place of production and not by entity, EU-manufactured FPV components are covered by the same listing as Chinese ones unless the Department of War or DHS grants a specific determination. Reshoring to Europe does not by itself solve US market access. https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-25-1086A1.pdf [verified] (primary text, reading is mine)
- Of the eleven manufacturers holding FCC Conditional Approvals as of 4 June 2026, at least Verity AG (Switzerland), Mobilicom (Israel/Australia) and ScoutDI (Norway) are non-US, so the exemption route is open to allies in practice. https://dronedj.com/2026/06/09/fcc-drone-ban-exempt-list/ [single]
- Neros Technologies, El Segundo California, is the clearest example of an explicitly FPV-focused non-Chinese manufacturer. It raised a 75 million USD Series B in November 2025 led by Sequoia Capital with Vy Capital US and Interlagos, bringing total funding above 120 million USD. https://dronedj.com/ [single] (multiple outlets, figures are company-announced)
- Neros holds a US Army contract reported at up to 500 million USD through 2031 for its Archer FPV system, and its product is described as built without Chinese components and approved under the Pentagon's Blue UAS programme. Company and contract claims, not independently audited. [single]
- Neros states a target of one million drones annually by 2028. COMPANY CLAIM, target not output. [single]
- I could not verify: EU Drone Strategy 2.0 and EDIP funding amounts, the 2025 European "drone wall" proposals, China's share of global NdFeB magnet output with a citable figure and year, Blue UAS list size and the cost multiple versus Chinese parts, Section 301 tariff rates and the end of the de minimis exemption, and whether any European company makes FPV-class motors, ESCs, VTXs or goggles rather than assembling Chinese parts. That last one is the crux question for this section and it remains open. [could not verify]
